What is simplified in this model?

Optical paths are schematic and not to scale. Disk rotation uses a 4,000 rpm reference, converted exactly by the chosen display slowdown; this is a reference operating point, not the speed of every instrument. Light rays are schematic. The 10 ms camera exposure and 5 ms hold share the slowed rotation clock. The comparison below uses equal arbitrary dwell times with 1 versus 8 spots, not predicted hardware frame rates. Lens/pinhole counts, spot sizes, cell structure, depth and exposure timing are illustrative, with no calibrated instrument units. The objective represents the relay/objective system; the exact relay train is omitted. The camera uses a synthetic fluorescent phantom and a scan-coverage mask, not a diffraction calculation or acquired data. The ray comparison uses a paraxial thin-lens model and a circular Gaussian footprint to illustrate aperture transmission. The widefield/confocal comparison uses synthetic depth-dependent blur and Gaussian section weighting, not a measured PSF. Brightness is in shared arbitrary units. Pinhole crosstalk, noise, bleaching and aberrations are omitted. In thick or scattering specimens, out-of-focus light can enter neighboring pinholes and reduce section contrast; optical sectioning does not imply unlimited penetration depth. Depth is controlled independently of disk rotation.
